# Metal Gear (1995) — Complete Campaign Character Narrative and Timeline
## Operation Intrude N313 · First-playthrough narrative architecture · 21 November 2026

> **Scope:** Only events during or immediately framing the **1995 Outer Heaven uprising and this game**. This is a comprehensive **narrative specification**, not 105 implemented scenes. The dates of individual events, hours of infiltration, exact age/height of most supporting actors, off-screen deaths and named identities of ordinary soldiers are not established by the 1987 source and must not be fabricated.

### Canon and source discipline

1. **Target-specific authority:** the selected Connect2Media J2ME v3.3.0 a1 *Metal Gear* game, once the actual event and resource formats have been decoded and checked in a running version. We have *not* proven its room/event/dialogue map.
2. **Original game comparison:** 1987 MSX2 Japanese original first; historical English MSX2 script, official Konami profiles and maps as comparison, with localization differences recorded. Do not import NES redesigns as original architecture.
3. **Later-canon context:** MG2, MGS and MGSV establish the 1995 date and identity consequences. Such knowledge may shape production subtext but **not become omniscient exposition for the 1995 characters**.
4. **Remake invention:** fine-grained dialogue, acting, environmental artifacts, personal reactions, guard chatter, optional memories, facades and transitions are proposed, never passed off as established dialogue.
5. **No fictional precise schedule:** the canonical mission has a sequence, not documented day-by-day times. A player can explore or revisit rooms in varying order. The 40 numbered scenes describe critical dramatic logic, not a fixed cinematic playlist.

## Individually fleshed 1995 narrative arcs

### Solid Snake — novice, witness, disobedient survivor

**Opening:** Newly recruited to FOXHOUND, with Big Boss as superior. He is not the stoic omniscient veteran of later games. Establish competence at basic infiltration but limited confidence in a sprawling unknown fortress. The player learns as he learns. Big Boss's opening radio must be helpful and restrained, not villainously theatrical.

**Building One:** Equipment acquisition, stealth, prisoner rescue and finding new radio voices make Snake a competent operator. His moral decisions are shown in actions: stopping for a cell while patrols are still active, protecting a captured source, not automatically firing at anyone who moves. Optional humor and repeated incredulous questions can remain character flavour but should not weaken his intelligence.

**Gray Fox:** Deliberately surrendering control to infiltrate a secret prison is the pivotal early act. Beside a captured senior operator, Snake is no longer merely the rookie receiving orders. Fox's truth shifts the objective from reconnaissance to preventing a global nuclear threat and finding the scientist.

**Madnar and Ellen:** When the engineer refuses to discuss the TX-55 while his daughter is held, Snake could treat this as obstruction, but the mission instead calls on him to save her. Bringing Ellen's rescue confirmation back to the scientist, by actually traversing changed spaces, establishes Snake as someone whose value cannot be reduced to operational efficiency.

**Betrayal:** Big Boss first gives good advice, then false directions at trucks and doors. Schneider's interrupted call and a surviving witness's explicit disclosure give Snake independent evidence. The defining character action is continuing despite his commander's direct abort order.

**Finale:** Using Madnar's remembered sequence to destroy TX-55 is a deliberate player act; then, under a real self-destruct countdown, Snake survives the commander's armed betrayal. He remembers the left-ladder advice Jennifer's brother supplied earlier. Escape is a consequence of people he chose to help. He reports completion; what he believes about the identity of the man he defeated must remain limited to 1995.

**Acting:** Reserved rather than emotionless. Growing skepticism is expressed through delayed compliance, terse questioning and more independent radio choices, not retrospective speeches about Big Boss's ideology. **No:** experience with Liquid, REX, Shadow Moses or future genetic revelations.

### Gray Fox / Grey Fox — the brilliant operative whose capture starts the story

**Immediately before Snake's mission:** He carries FOXHOUND's prestigious FOX title. Infiltrates Outer Heaven first; his last known contact mentions Metal Gear. His exact capture choreography is **not shown in the original**. We can show a restrained fragment of a lost transmission or dossier, but inventing a fully choreographed canon flashback would be a new scene.

**First half of game:** Prisoners and environmental evidence point toward his confinement. The prison's deliberate-capture route means Snake physically approaches the condition in which Fox is held: disarmed and uncertain. Fox should read as exhausted but alert; captivity does not erase his ability to assess danger.

**The rescue moment:** Fox identifies nuclear-capable Metal Gear and names the engineer as the person who can explain its destruction. He is an indispensable expert witness, **not** a boss or collectible. Deliver the core lead in a concise, interruptible exchange with an always-available intel log.

**After leaving the cell:** His story's 1995 source contribution is complete; where precisely he goes and what he does for the rest of this mission is not documented. A brief optional line acknowledging Snake's willingness to be captured may enrich the moment **as remake-only**, but a mandatory escape buddy, 1995 cyborg ninja transformation, assassin return or later boss fight would falsify the game. The aftermath is his influence: Snake takes his knowledge to Madnar and prevents the threat.

**Later-continuity production note:** His future loyalty conflicts and cyborg fate belong to **post-1995** works; they may inform designers about emotional ambiguity but not be prematurely played out here.

### Big Boss, FOXHOUND commander — useful teacher, covert betrayer

**Before 1995 contact:** He publicly commands FOXHOUND. The same identity is connected to the Outer Heaven scheme. The 1987 plot wants Snake to believe the voice is his mission's legitimate command.

**Beginning:** Big Boss provides actual mission orders, gear knowledge, clues about resistance, doors, gas masks and equipment recovery. These should work. A villain who only lies from the start makes the late reveal meaningless.

**Middle:** He continues sounding like a real superior, changes transceiver channel in response to an operational problem, and gives checkpoint advice. Retain credible radio protocol and periods of silence. Snake should not infer treachery merely from an eerie music cue.

**After Madnar's disclosure:** False truck and door guidance becomes materially dangerous. The player can test those directions, so **the trust problem is playable**. Disinformation should be specific and avoid forced scripted damage; knowledgeable players should evade it.

**Near the core:** A prisoner identifies him as Outer Heaven's leader, and Big Boss's radio message escalates from subtle manipulation to direct demand that Snake stop. The original platform-breaking command to turn off the system should be adapted thoughtfully for modern platforms rather than literally forcing a quit.

**Climax:** The physical commander tells Snake he was expected to bring back misleading information and had accomplished too much. Snake fights the man he believes to be Big Boss. The closing threat after the credits maintains the unresolved legacy.

**Crucial later-canon distinction:** *MGSV* identifies the **physical Outer Heaven commander Snake defeats** as **Venom Snake, Big Boss's phantom**, while the true Big Boss is the FOXHOUND commander directing the operation. The 1987 text does not tell Snake this. The modern remake must preserve the original scene's perceived betrayal and keep the body-double distinction as optional retrospective material, if exposed at all. Do **not** retroactively tell Snake he knew the deception during this game.

### Venom Snake — the phantom behind the 1995 fortress identity

**Visible 1995 role:** The physical leader at Outer Heaven, publicly taking the Big Boss identity. He protects the fortress until TX-55's destruction. His precise offscreen movements, conversations and internal thoughts during Intrude N313 are *not documented* by the original.

**Player experience:** The figure at the end must first read to Snake as his commander, not a second unfamiliar villain. No early in-game caption identifying the phantom. Character rig, scars, voice and face should be continuity-reviewed so later-canon truth is possible without making first-time players deduce it through an obvious art mistake.

**End:** Defeated during the final confrontation, the fortress collapsing. The true commander's continued existence remains unknown to Snake here. No newly written direct radio exchange between the two Big Boss identities is canonical.

### Kyle Schneider — grieving architect, operational resistance leader

**Established pregame:** An architect involved in Outer Heaven construction; his wife and child were killed; he became a resistance leader. That tragedy is his reason to fight, not an excuse to invent additional atrocities. The fortress itself carries evidence of his former work.

**S01–S02:** His advice about gas masks, secret routes, modified walls and prisoner corridors is practical. Let a player notice inconsistent wall construction before any call; Schneider confirms rather than solves all mysteries. His voice should sound technically precise and personally restrained.

**S03–S04:** Provides building/crossing/underground link knowledge. Return routes should reveal the literal correctness of his construction knowledge. If he warns about danger, that danger must exist in the rendered level.

**S05:** Calls when he thinks he has learned who controls the fortress. The transmission cuts off before completion. Original mission does not definitively show his death or capture, so do not stage either as canon. His sudden absence should be meaningful but not a fabricated new mandatory subplot.

**Closure:** Player cannot save him in a new side-mission unless explicitly approved as a remake departure. Leave the fate unresolved for this game. **Do not** reveal his later MG2 identity in 1995.

### Diane and Steve — the resistance as actual people

**Diane before mission:** A former singer from the band Thin Wall and an expert in Outer Heaven's mercenary personnel. She is not a standard FOXHOUND tactician.

**Early:** A prisoner supplies her channel. Her usefulness is distinctly about enemy behaviour: why a heavy shooter commits to fire, where mines counter a tank, why an apparently invincible defender demands another tool. Write lines from knowledge of mercenaries, not video-game tutorial omniscience.

**Middle:** She reacts to the real dangers of Snake's route; allow her to be unavailable at times without breaking the game's controls. In some Japanese MSX2 radio variants **Steve** answers Diane's frequency, complaining she is out. Subsequent canon identifies him as her brother. The European localization omitted his calls. Treat him as a minor **variant-gated optional voice**, not a mandatory in-facility NPC.

**Final:** During the Big Boss encounter she admits she doesn't know the answer, and nearly confesses affection before awkwardly retreating. Preserve the poignancy and her lack of certainty; this is a limited source emotional beat, **not** proof of a committed romantic relationship or grounds for new marriage/romance scenes.

**Closure:** Diane and Steve's survival and future lives are not given a specific scene in the 1987 game. Do not invent them.

### Jennifer and her brother — clandestine help becomes personal

**Established pregame:** Jennifer joined Outer Heaven's medical staff undercover to find her imprisoned brother. She has a reason to risk her life, unlike a generic item dispenser.

**S03–S04:** Prisoner rescues lead Snake to her channel. In the original MSX2, she will answer only when Snake has earned a sufficient rank through rescue; her intervention helps secure the rocket launcher and open a protected door, enabling progress around TX-11 androids and the compass. Her credibility is demonstrated through a real, accessible item or opened door, not a magically appearing inventory entry. **This radio is a source-required mechanical intervention**, not merely optional exposition. A player must not be soft-locked by having missed a permanently inaccessible POW.

**S05:** She helps find an oxygen cylinder across damaged architecture and warns about Coward Duck, who holds her brother among other captives. Her request to spare prisoners should be explicit *before* the player first commits a damaging attack. The boss fight must represent hostage positions and danger accurately, not use invisible failure flags.

**Her brother:** He is unnamed in the source. Rescue him from Coward Duck's hostage group; he identifies himself and tells Snake that the **left ladder** is the safe evacuation route. His role starts as Jennifer's private goal and ends as the reason Snake makes the right escape choice. Do not give him an invented military rank or dangerous combat powers.

**Final:** Jennifer urges Snake to flee and offers a practical note about extending the timer with cigarettes (source detail; evaluate gameplay/accessibility and safety presentation). She cannot know that Snake killed her brother; the encounter must record real survival state. An optional short relieved acknowledgement after his safe rescue is a **remake line**, not a source reunion.

### Drago Pettrovich Madnar — coerced maker and terrified father

**Before mission:** Scientist fleeing toward the United States with his daughter Ellen; Outer Heaven captured them. The regime holds Ellen to force his weapons engineering. Konami identifies his **TX-11 Arnold** units and **TX-55 Metal Gear**.

**Early:** Fox names him as the only source of how to stop Metal Gear. False location intelligence and a hostile trap delay Snake. Make it believable that a security organisation would hide its engineer; never imply Madnar personally constructed a false captive to deceive Snake.

**First meeting:** He refuses to reveal how to sabotage TX-55 until Ellen is released. This is a father protecting his daughter under coercion, not a villain deliberately withholding information out of pride. The core dialogue is short and physically located in his actual Building Two cell. No early exposition dump describing future Zanzibar Land.

**After Ellen:** Snake makes the long return, an act with emotional cost to the player. Pettrovich gives the machine's deep Building Three location and a specific sequence of **16 plastic explosive charges** on its two feet, with the last step absent from the original speech. The player should be able to record/replay what he said. Do not invent a convenient neural uplink or automatically resolve the final action.

**End:** His cooperation leads directly to the destruction of the device. The game does not portray a complete family reunion cutscene, detailed post-extraction fate or later career. **Later MG2 motivations are future continuity, not his covert intent in this game.**

### Ellen Pettrovich — hidden hostage, independent witness

**Before mission:** The scientist's daughter and former Bolshoi Ballet star. Captured alongside him; she is the regime's leverage. There is no source evidence she is a combat operative.

**While Snake is elsewhere:** The player can learn she is missing from Madnar; never intercut to fictional torture scenes as if canonical. A distant cry or environmental clue on the earlier floor may foreshadow her actual position *without* allowing premature scripted rescue.

**Rescue:** In **Building One basement**, after passing actual gas, dogs, pits and doors, Snake hears a cry beyond a weak wall and breaks into a hidden cell. It is dramatically resonant that the same fortress system which imprisoned Fox has now separated a daughter from her father. She identifies herself and confirms his forced work. This is the powerful core of her arc.

**End:** She urges Snake to help her father. He returns to Madnar; her own exact extraction path is not depicted. A short on-screen acknowledgement that she is safe is a proposed local closure, not permission to teleport her into a reunion cutscene or redefine the scientist's conditions.

### The other prisoners — witnesses rather than collectibles

Early captives supply operational context; some identify secret cells and deliberate capture. Middle captives identify equipment, rank requirements, the real scientist, Jennifer and the travel corridors. A late captive in Building Three reveals Big Boss's command. Each *source-critical* prisoner must have a stable identity/location, one durable intel message and explicit rescue flag; unnamed prisoners need not be assigned invented names or fixed careers. Their rescue can build rank without demanding 100% completion, and rescuing them should never cause unmotivated world-state resets.

### Specialist soldiers and hostile personnel

**Shoot Gunner / Shotmaker:** Source-name prison-area shotgun enforcer, described in manuals as ex-Spetsnaz. He is the first vivid hostile personality after Gray Fox's rescue: a man who believes confinement cannot be escaped. Build a distinctive burst/spread and lengthy commitment, exploiting existing cover, without claiming a secret childhood, orders from Big Boss or a tragic personal past.

**Machinegun Kid:** Described as a former SAS soldier; specialises in suppressive fire. His function is spatial pressure and careful flanking. Actor work should make weapon mass, fire discipline and recovery legible. Do not invent an actual minor's age from the codename.

**Fire Trooper:** Described as ex-GSG9, using a flamethrower in a protected interior. The enclosure tells his role; fuel/heat consequences distinguish his fight. Do not attribute a known real-world mission to him without evidence.

**Coward Duck / Dirty Duck:** Boomerang-armed mercenary with an extremist-group background in original supplementary material; holds prisoners as human shields, carries Card 8. His cowardice is portrayed *mechanically*: positioning himself behind people whom Snake wants alive. This fight is Jennifer's personal climax. Defeating him without killing hostages should be both possible and rewarding.

**TX-11 Arnold / Bloody Brad:** Two android enforcers built by Madnar, not people with biographies. Their unusual durability teaches that the scientist's machines have different vulnerabilities. Jennifer's rocket assistance is a major lead-in. Card 7 allows further movement.

**False scientist trap:** A misleading Pettrovich location is part of the source route. Do not insist the deception is a named doppelgänger or give a fabricated biography. Design it as a deceptive detention location or announcement whose truth the player can inspect.

**Hind D, tank and bulldozer crews:** Unnamed operators (if shown at all), not named new bosses. Their stories are environmental assignments: airspace, exterior access and road denial. Threats should be foreshadowed by hangars, maintenance, acoustic approach and readable routes. Boss order follows MSX2 comparison, but exact physical layouts still require J2ME tracing.

**Ordinary guards and staff:** They operate an inhabited facility, communicate sightings and secure real things: trucks, lifts, restricted research areas, cells and bomb hazards. Some world building can show fatigue or divided loyalties as **remake interpretation**. Do not imply all soldiers knew the commander's hidden identity, nor fabricate new canonical atrocities.

### TX-55 Metal Gear — machine as the last nonhuman protagonist of the plot

The machine's arc is the thread joining everyone: the last word of Fox's report; the object Snake is ordered to find; the result of Madnar's coerced work; Ellen's reason for captivity; the strategic purpose of Outer Heaven; finally a destructible military system with a *particular* sabotage procedure. It is **not** a sapient villain, a REX, or a multi-stage 1990s autonomous boss. Its source destruction is a material, spatial, sixteen-action objective that kicks off the final self-destruct countdown.

## Endgame order, precisely

1. Jennifer's brother rescued; **left escape ladder** clue obtained.
2. Basement prisoner explicitly reveals Big Boss as Outer Heaven commander, and Big Boss issues **stop operation** order.
3. Snake reaches TX-55 with the earned equipment, information and explosive stock.
4. **TX-55 destruction via 16 foot-charge placements occurs first.**
5. Self-destruct countdown begins.
6. Final commander appearing as Big Boss declares Snake spoiled his plan; Diane's emotional farewell occurs around final encounter.
7. Player defeats the commander; escape shaft opens.
8. Snake follows remembered **left ladder** and exits, optionally hearing Jennifer's practical warning.
9. Snake reports mission success, fortress explodes; closing message implies danger is not finished.

The original's source-specified countdown and last-ladder knowledge make these separate episodes emotionally linked. The **MGSV body-double retcon** is only later-continuity framing; it should not be inserted as a pre-finale cinematic explaining itself to Snake.

## Exact presentation and gameplay requirements

Each source scene needs a stable event key, an area/floor anchor, its source comparison, a pre-state and post-state, an interruptible dialogue/animation cue, a no-audio equivalent, and save/reload verification. A player who never dials an *optional* resistance line must still be able to find a real item/door and proceed. However, **Jennifer's source-required mechanical requests** (launcher and certain access) can be legitimate progression interactions; their POW/rank prerequisites must be clear and recoverable.

For replayable narrative:
- **Never auto-play a lengthy cutscene when the player walks past a relevant marker.** Trigger direct interaction, respect stealth combat and preserve full transcripts.
- **Keep emotional scenes short at first contact**, with optional follow-on lines that unlock only when the source event is safe and already complete.
- **Write reactive state, not alternate canon:** when a player reaches a location early, information and dialogue must reflect what Snake genuinely knows.
- **Separate actor survival and mission gates:** hostages matter, especially in Coward Duck's arena, without letting an accidental projectile permanently corrupt the whole campaign. Source-consistent fail and recovery paths require actual UX testing.
- **No unsupported flashbacks of Venom, Fox, Schneider or Madnar.** A dramatic scene can evoke their history with environmental design or a readable dossier without fabricating a canonical earlier mission.
- **Backtracking changes places for reasons:** guard duty, opened doors, recovered inventory, rescued people and real structural damage persist across revisits.
- **Cinematic and codec fidelity:** show period-appropriate transceiver, distinct 1995 outfits/poses, performed faces/voices when obtained, and strong spatial audio. No later-era implanted Codec.
